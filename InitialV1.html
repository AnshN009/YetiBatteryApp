voltage statistics

How to read these graphs

Raw voltage (faint) shows every sample. 0.1 s rolling minimum (~5 samples at 50 Hz) catches individual brownout spikes without smoothing them away. The radio brownouts at 6.3 V. CAN controllers fault below ~6.5 V. Motor torque drops meaningfully below 8.5 V. Jitter in the rolling-min trace = high-frequency current transients from your drivetrain or shooter.

current statistics

Reading current graphs

FRC 18 Ah SLA batteries sustain ~18 A continuously and burst to ~200 A before voltage collapses. The main breaker trips at 120 A sustained (with a time-delay curve — short spikes above 120 A are okay). Sustained current above 80–100 A depletes the battery quickly and raises internal temperature. Peaks above 150 A on a degraded battery are primary brownout triggers. Stagger simultaneous actuations to reduce peak current.

power statistics

Reading power graphs

Power (W) = V × I. A healthy FRC match sees 400–1000 W average with peaks to 2500 W. Total energy consumed tells you what fraction of the battery's ~216 Wh (18 Ah × 12 V) capacity you used. Using more than 70% in one match means the second consecutive match will have a noticeably weaker battery. A rising rolling-mean power curve means your robot is demanding more power as voltage drops (sag forces higher current to maintain torque).

impedance statistics

What internal resistance means

R = (V_oc − V) / I. Computed for every sample where I > 15 A. A fresh 18 Ah SLA sits at 8–12 mΩ. At 30 mΩ, a 120 A pull drops voltage by 3.6 V — enough to trigger radio brownout from nominal 12.0 V.

  • Rising trend over match → battery heating internally or depleting — resistance increases as plates sulfate under load
  • Sharp isolated spikes → simultaneous high-current actuations — stagger them in code
  • High median, few spikes → uniformly aged battery — replace before competition
  • Wide histogram tail → frequent transient spikes — design/code issue

BFG sensor statistics

About REV BFG (Battery Function Gauge) data

The REV BFG communicates over CAN and provides hardware-measured battery metrics that are more accurate than estimates derived from voltage/current math alone. Key fields: voltage (V), current (A), temperature (°C), state of charge (%), internal resistance (mΩ), full capacity (Ah), remaining capacity (Ah), and cycle count. Temperature is especially important — resistance rises sharply when the battery is cold (<15°C) and again when it overheats (>45°C). The BFG's measured resistance is more accurate than the V/I estimation method used in the Impedance tab.

  • Rising temperature + rising resistance → battery heating under repeated discharge — let it rest between matches
  • High cycle count + high resistance → end of useful life — replace
  • Remaining capacity much less than full capacity → capacity fade — battery can't deliver its rated energy
  • SOC drops faster than expected → high self-discharge or internal short — retire the battery
